# Slide 2 Drilldown

Slide 1 establishes the overall industry view. Slide 2 should go one level deeper into the strongest structural factor that makes the market understandable: a segment split, customer structure, channel shift, benchmark, profit pool, policy change, or competitive structure.

Choose the drilldown from the evidence, not from a fixed industry template. The best Slide 2 adds a new metric set or structural axis and gives the reader a sharper way to read the market.

Useful directions include:

- market segmentation: category sizes, sub-market growth, or profit differences;
- customer structure: customer groups, concentration, retention, willingness to pay, or spend per customer;
- channel structure: direct/distributor/platform mix, channel economics, or go-to-market change;
- application scenarios: end-use mix, emerging use cases, or application-led demand shift;
- value-chain profit pool: high-level value distribution and margin pools, leaving detailed mechanics for Slide 4;
- penetration and benchmark: adoption rate, mature-market benchmark, headroom, or catch-up potential;
- technology or product evolution: product generations, substitution, R&D intensity, or architecture change;
- policy and regulation: policy changes that reshape economics, barriers, compliance cost, or access;
- supply-demand dynamics: capacity, utilization, pricing, inventory, or cycle balance;
- competitive structure: concentration, player types, share shifts, or market-structure change, leaving detailed peer comparison for Slide 6.

Avoid repeating Slide 1 with different wording. If the best drilldown is not supported by evidence, use a source-boundary exhibit rather than forcing a weak chart.
